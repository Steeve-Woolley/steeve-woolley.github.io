About

Steeve Woolley is a Canadian oil painter, born in Cap-Haïtien, Haiti.

Before he was seven, he spent his days in his cousin's sign-painting studio, watching storefront signs, murals, and lettering come to life by hand. In 1990s Haiti everything was painted, and those hours — paint meeting surface, an image appearing where there had been none — shaped how he understood image-making long before he had words for it.

After moving to Canada he built a career in animation, a form of visual storytelling that has run alongside his private practice in oil ever since. It has left him with an animator's instinct for light, for composition, and for the narrative weight a single arrested moment can carry.

Light is his subject. Particularly the kind that arrives at one hour of the day and makes a familiar space briefly unfamiliar — a room at golden hour, a wall catching the late afternoon, the geometry of somewhere seen a hundred times made strange by the angle of the sun. He works from observation, from architecture, from memory, and from the classical painters he returns to in his favorite museums.

He lives and works in Hamilton, Ontario.
